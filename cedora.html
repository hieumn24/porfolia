The setup

Cedora sells timber furniture in Australia on Shopify. 128 active products, 61 collections, and in February a blog with ten pages that ranked for anything at all. The handover numbers were not real: January shows 988 clicks on 4,694 impressions, a 21.05% click-through rate at average position 11.88, and 7,466 Direct sessions. By March, Direct was 1,121 and clicks were 253. I started in February. The first job was working out which half of the baseline to throw away, because every target in the contract had been set against the inflated half.

Where I decided not to fight

  • The client's range names are Liverpool, Byron, Richmond and Oxford. Nobody searches for them. Collection targeting went to the generic category terms instead: bedside table, tallboy, tv unit, dining sets.
  • Stopped reporting the blog as a revenue line and started reporting it as reach. It takes 1,916 of July's 2,217 page-level clicks and produced one order in the last sixty days. The client did not enjoy that slide.
  • Every recommendation gets cut on country = aus before it counts. Australia is 39% of clicks and the store ships nowhere else, so the headline number overstates commercial value by roughly two and a half times.
  • Did not chase the impression figure. Raw July impressions were 1,481,957. I reported 280,427 and explained the difference before anyone opened Search Console and found the gap themselves.
  • Product pages carry the highest organic order value on the site, and they were the least optimised thing in the account. Work moved there from the blog in July.

Technical work

  • One query, "how to measure room for furniture", put 1,201,530 impressions into July against 4 clicks. Brazil 198,768, India 109,135, Australia not in the top six. Desktop was 94.7% of it, which is backwards for a "how to" query. It went quiet in June and came back at 552,452 in the first eleven days of August.
  • Built the subtraction into the weekly pull rather than the report, so the aggregate, the daily series and the keyword sheets all use the same cleaned figure. Never use a notContains filter for this. It silently drops the anonymised rows and the total quietly changes underneath you.
  • Query-level clicks add up to 841 against the site total of 5,007 over the same 90 days. Two different layers of the same data. Anyone adding them together will publish a wrong number.
  • A headless scraper on an 800x600 viewport put 344 sessions into July, 340 of them landing in Direct, then 502 more in the first fortnight of August. Direct is half of the KPI, so it is filtered out at pull time.
  • The KPI sheet I shipped in July had two defects that were mine. The "organic traffic" row actually measured organic plus direct, so July had already beaten the December target on the day it was signed. The percentage column also coloured rows green while showing 66%. Rebuilt as five direct measures on 12 August.
  • Reporting is two Python scripts against the GA4 and Search Console APIs, anchored to the last fully final Search Console day rather than to today. Search Console runs about three days behind. Anchor to today and every week looks like a crash.

What organic is worth here

Shopify order journeys, 60 days to 15 Aug 2026. Shown as shares because the store is named.

  • Organic search is the first touch on 7.5% of revenue and appears somewhere in the journey on 10.8%. In order count that is a single-digit number over sixty days.
  • Those orders run 9% above the site's average order value. Small channel, better basket.
  • Paid search is the first touch on 37% of revenue and Direct on 33%. This is an ads-led store. SEO is not going to change that ratio this year and I have not told anyone it will.
  • The average gap between the organic click and the order is 11 days, longest 29. Any last-click report will undercount this channel, which is the argument I use for keeping the blog funded.
  • Read the traffic chart and this card together. Clicks went up 571%. The revenue share did not move proportionally, and pretending otherwise would be the easiest lie on this page.

What is still open

The keyword count is not the win it looks like. Of the 444 Australian clicks Search Console shows me at query level over 90 days, 241 are the brand: "cedora furniture" at position 1.0, "cedora" at 1.0, "cedora furniture reviews" at 1.2, plus the misspellings sedora, cendora and cedore. The other 10,295 keywords produced 203 clicks between them. Ranking at 5.1 for "tallboy meaning" is not the same as selling a tallboy.

198 of those Australian queries are not people. They are assistant prompts pasted into search, every one opening with "context: location: australia (not for language). do not include location references in your response." 194 rank in the top 10. All of them have zero clicks. I do not yet know whether that is an early channel or a mirage, and I would rather say so than build a slide around it.

Engagement got worse while traffic grew. Organic sessions ran 68.7 seconds in February and 60.2 in July, with engaged sessions falling from 89% to 70%. That is what winning informational long-tail does. I raised it before the KPI was signed rather than after it was missed.

Collections are the pages that sell and they have barely moved: 89 clicks in July against the blog's 1,916. On-page is finished on both. The next lever is authority and merchandising, not another forty articles.

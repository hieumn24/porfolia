The setup

SIAM Thailand is one of Thailand's leading aesthetic hospitals, specializing in cosmetic surgery and beauty treatments. When I joined as SEO Leader at XMARS Agency the website was starting from zero organic traffic, no backlink profile and no domain authority, in one of the most competitive verticals in the country. The brief was to build the whole thing and to do it inside 6 to 12 months.

Goals

  • Build organic traffic from 0 to 45,000+ daily visits
  • Establish SIAM as a top authority in the beauty and aesthetic industry
  • Reduce dependency on paid advertising
  • Create a sustainable organic customer acquisition channel
  • Handle and prevent SEO crisis situations

What I owned

  • Full website management and SEO operations
  • Technical SEO audits and optimization
  • Content strategy and implementation
  • Link building and off-page SEO
  • Crisis management: blackhat, hacking, DMCA

What made it hard

Six constraints that shaped every decision on this project.

Starting from absolute zero

No organic traffic, no backlink profile, no domain authority. Everything had to be built from scratch.

A brutal vertical

Aesthetic surgery in Thailand is dominated by established players holding the high-value keywords.

E-A-T requirements

Medical content has to demonstrate expertise, authoritativeness and trust, especially after the Medic update.

Two audiences

Thai and international visitors needed separate keyword and content strategies.

Active attacks

Repeated blackhat attacks, hacking attempts and DMCA reports aimed at the site's rankings.

An aggressive clock

The client expected results inside 6 to 12 months to justify cutting paid spend.

Technical overhaul

  • In-depth audit identifying 200+ issues
  • Structured data for medical content and reviews
  • Load time from 4.2s down to 1.8s
  • Fixed crawlability, robots.txt and XML sitemaps
  • Mobile-first responsive optimization
  • Monitoring through Search Console and Analytics

Content

  • 500+ medically accurate articles on aesthetic procedures
  • Content clusters around each service category
  • E-A-T signals through author credentials and citations
  • Before and after galleries with proper schema
  • Thai and international versions of key content
  • Optimized for featured snippets and rich results

Authority building

  • High-authority links from medical and beauty publications
  • Relationships with healthcare influencers and bloggers
  • Linkable assets: research, infographics, guides
  • PR and media coverage for natural link acquisition
  • Strategic internal linking structure

Crisis and security

  • Security hardening to prevent repeat attacks
  • Round-the-clock monitoring for blackhat tactics
  • Rapid response protocols for DMCA and hacking incidents
  • Defended and resolved 5+ major attacks
  • Held rankings through the crisis periods

The journey: from crisis to breakthrough

This happened after the KPI was met, not on the way to it. In February 2025 the site was compromised and every ranking went to zero. Five months to get it back.

The darkest day, February 2025

It was February. I woke up to a nightmare, my inbox flooded with alerts from Search Console and Google Analytics. The website had been severely hacked. All keyword rankings dropped to zero. Traffic suddenly showed thousands of strange, unknown pages.

Sitting in front of my screen, all my previous SEO knowledge felt meaningless. The client was calling. My team was looking at me, waiting for solutions. The pressure was indescribable. "This is it," I thought. "We're done."

The first weeks, firefighting

First, I had to deal with extremely powerful malware. The website was being redirected to Japanese phone sales pages. 30,000 spam links were being fired continuously. Even worse, all official pages lost their index, leaving only hacked pages in Google's collection. The original sitemap disappeared, replaced by a fake sitemap full of garbage links.

The IT team didn't grasp the issue, so they were slow to respond. Every second that passed was another second of reputation damage. I was forced to teach myself, clean every line of code myself, rebuild from scratch. Sleepless nights weren't just from worry, they were from sitting and dissecting every file to find deeply hidden malware.

The struggle, months four and five

By this time, traffic had started to recover, but we still couldn't reach our goals. The project timeline was almost up, and despite trying every tactic, the signals weren't coming back. I started questioning myself: where did I go wrong?

Night after night, I analyzed everything from the beginning, checking every metric, every change I'd made. At times, I genuinely doubted my methods. My previous experience didn't seem to apply to this case. It was completely different.

Someone once told me: step out of your comfort zone, and you'll learn a lot. Back then, I just nodded along, thinking I understood. But the reality was completely different from what I'd imagined.

Before, doing SEO in a team meant having support, backup plans, and shared responsibility. Now I had to take responsibility for everything, from technical to strategy, from client reports to explaining to my boss why rankings weren't improving yet. I had to know everything, and there was nobody to check my answer against.

The breakthrough, July 2025

One morning in July I woke up to a notification from Google: you reached a new traffic high in the last 30 days. My first thought was, what's this? Are we hacked again? Heart pounding, hands shaking, I opened Search Console.

Checked the rankings and there it was. The main keyword had reached top 3. I sat there refreshing several times, thinking Google must be glitching. But no, it was real.

Early July, I thought we were stable, but then we dropped again due to hidden spam links still being fired. At that moment, I felt truly desperate. Then, at the end of July, the real breakthrough came, and after it the domino effect.

Invaluable lessons

Technical foundation is everythingWithout a solid foundation, everything else is useless.
In a crisis, don't panicStick to basic principles, take it step by step.
User experience isn't a sloganGoogle truly favors pages that genuinely solve user problems.
Patience in SEO isn't optionalIt's mandatory.

Looking back

Five months ago, I thought my career would have a huge black mark. Today, this has become my proudest project.

Not because of the results, but because of the entire journey. Because of the sleepless nights, the moments of wanting to give up but gritting my teeth and continuing.

SEO isn't just about rankings. It's about resilience, about never giving up.
